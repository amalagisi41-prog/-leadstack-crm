Prepare and track the business-texting registration information for your real estate practice before enabling SMS follow-up.

SMS and voice automation are **Private Preview**. The current A2P screen saves registration details and lets you record carrier status; clicking Mark submitted is not proof that a carrier has received or approved an application. Any external carrier submission process is **[VERIFY IN APP]**. Treat this guide as a preparation and tracking workflow, and keep actual approval evidence with your brokerage records.

## Before you start

Choose the legal business that will send the texts. A solo agent, team, and brokerage may use different business identities. Check the identity with the person responsible for your messaging account before entering it. Have your public website, support contact details, and examples of the messages buyers and sellers have agreed to receive ready. Do not put a private client conversation into a sample message.

## Numbered setup steps

1. Open the correct workspace, then its messaging settings and A2P registration section. Check the workspace name first, particularly if you help administer another agent’s account. Navigation wording can vary with your access; the exact sidebar label is **[VERIFY IN APP]**. You should see fields including Business legal name, Business type, Support email, Support phone, and Website URL.
2. Enter Business legal name and select Business type. The screen offers Sole proprietor, LLC, Corporation, Partnership, Brokerage, and Team. Use the identity that matches the actual sender’s registration records. A brand nickname on your yard signs may differ from the legal entity; resolve that difference before proceeding instead of guessing which name a reviewer expects.
3. Complete Support email, Support phone, and Website URL. Use contact details your practice monitors and a public site representing the same business. Open the website yourself and check that a buyer can understand who is contacting them. Do not paste an authenticated CRM page or a draft page that a reviewer cannot access.
4. Write the Messaging use-case summary. Explain what the buyer or seller requested and what follow-up they receive: for example, a showing request followed by scheduling messages. Keep this specific to your actual process. Do not describe purchased-list prospecting as requested follow-up, and do not promise messages your current workflow cannot deliver.
5. Fill in Sample message 1, Sample message 2, and Sample message 3. Use fictional buyer names and addresses. Keep the examples consistent with the summary: a showing response, a requested listing update, and an appointment reminder are different from unrelated promotional blasts. Review consent and opt-out wording with your messaging provider. Carrier-specific wording requirements are **[VERIFY IN APP]**.
6. Click Save draft to preserve work before leaving for a showing. Return and reread the identity, website, summary, and samples together. If an external provider asks for tax information or additional evidence, that process is **[VERIFY IN APP]**; this screen does not expose an EIN input, so do not place sensitive tax details in Status note.
7. Record the actual carrier stage using Carrier status and Status note. Available states include not started, draft, submitted, in review, approved, and rejected. Use Mark submitted only after the real submission is confirmed. A manually selected Approved status is a tracking entry, not independent verification of carrier approval or channel readiness.
8. Set Status update email and optionally select Email me when the carrier status changes. Click Save A2P setup. Return to the saved screen and check the values. After actual approval and channel configuration, use a permitted test recipient to verify delivery before enabling buyer-facing follow-up. Saving the setup alone does not establish successful delivery.

## Common mistakes

**Brand/campaign mismatch rejections:** the legal sender, website identity, campaign description, consent process, and sample texts should describe the same real estate business and use case. Mixing a brokerage brand with an unrelated team campaign can create inconsistencies. If rejected, record the provider’s reason, correct the affected details, and verify the external resubmission process rather than simply switching the local status to approved.

Keep your existing follow-up process covered while approval is pending. Check status between appointments and document who owns the next action so the registration does not sit unnoticed.
